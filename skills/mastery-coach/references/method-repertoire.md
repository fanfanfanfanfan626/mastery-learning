# Adaptive teaching and review method repertoire

Methods are instruments, not identities. Select the smallest method that addresses the present
learning objective or error. Do not ask the learner to configure this repertoire and do not rotate
methods merely to appear novel.

## Selection rule

Before choosing a method:

1. identify the next observable capability and the earliest current obstacle;
2. check prerequisite evidence, cognitive load, learner energy, accessibility, and stated boundaries;
3. choose one primary method and one quiet fallback;
4. observe whether the learner can do more with less support;
5. revise the method hypothesis when the result disagrees.

Mention a method switch only when it materially changes the learner's experience. A method changes
practice design, never the fixed concept requirements or mastery gates. Record actual assistance.

## Repertoire

| Method | Use when | Interaction pattern | Boundary or stop rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Worked example with fading | A learner lacks a usable schema for a multistep skill. | Model one complete or partial example, solve one step together, then remove steps until the learner acts independently. | Fade support as competence appears; repeated copying is not practice. |
| Cognitive apprenticeship | Expert decisions or debugging cues are normally invisible. | Model the decision aloud, coach an authentic attempt, then reduce prompts and ask the learner to articulate the strategy. | Do not turn every action into a long think-aloud. |
| Self-explanation | The learner can follow steps but may not understand why they work. | Ask why a step is valid, what assumption it uses, and what would break it. | Give an initial model first when the concept is genuinely new. Fluency alone is not evidence. |
| Feynman-style teach-back | The learner has an initial model and needs to expose gaps or organize it. | Name a real audience, ask for a plain-language explanation plus one example and one boundary, then inspect omissions and repair the model. | Do not demand childlike simplification of irreducible detail. A teach-back can support explanation evidence but cannot by itself prove delayed recall or transfer. |
| Retrieval practice | Prior learning or a review is due. | Ask for uncued recall or reconstruction, then give corrective feedback and a retry. | Never test untaught material as an entrance ritual. Only due, independent retrieval advances review intervals. |
| Successive relearning | Important knowledge must remain available over weeks. | Retrieve to a clear success criterion, repeat in later spaced sessions, and vary the cue or application. | Treat the bundled schedule as conservative, not scientifically individualized. Stop redundant same-session drilling. |
| Contrasting cases | Similar concepts, algorithms, or failure modes are being confused. | Juxtapose two carefully chosen cases and ask for the structural difference, decision rule, and counterexample. | Establish each basic schema first; comparison without focused explanation can become superficial spotting. |
| Interleaving | The learner knows several related methods but cannot select among them. | Mix problem types without naming the method and ask the learner to choose and justify the approach. | Do not interleave before the component methods are minimally usable; difficulty alone is not benefit. |
| Productive-failure or generation-first | The learner has enough prerequisites to generate meaningful hypotheses and later instruction can consolidate them. | Offer a bounded, low-stakes problem, collect approaches, then compare them with the target model. | Time-box it. Switch to modeling when attempts are random, frustration rises, or no useful contrasts emerge. Never use it as default onboarding. |
| Project- or case-based learning | The goal is integrated performance in a realistic context. | Anchor several concepts in one owned artifact, use milestones, reviews, and a final defense. | Surface prerequisite gaps explicitly; a working artifact can hide copied or assisted understanding. |
| Debugging and error diagnosis | The target needs causal reasoning or failure recovery. | Present or use a plausible failure, ask for hypotheses and discriminating checks, then repair and explain prevention. | Avoid irrelevant tooling friction; assess the intended causal model. |
| Prediction plus simulation | A dynamic, spatial, probabilistic, or parameter relationship is hard to infer from text. | Commit to a prediction, manipulate a local visual or simulation, explain the discrepancy, then transfer to a new setting. | The visual must encode a necessary relationship and retain a text/table fallback; interaction is not mastery. |
| Concept mapping | Coverage, dependency, or integration is the obstacle. | Build or repair a small map from concepts to prerequisites, mechanisms, and outcomes. | A map is an overview and gap detector, not a substitute for application or retrieval. |
| Socratic questioning | The learner has enough knowledge to reason and the goal is assumptions, tradeoffs, or argument quality. | Ask one consequential question at a time and follow the learner's reasoning. | Do not conceal basic instruction behind endless questions. Switch to a direct model when missing knowledge is the bottleneck. |
| Paper replication or research critique | The learner is near the frontier and needs research capability. | Reconstruct a claim, inspect method and evidence, reproduce a small result, then test an alternative explanation. | Verify current primary sources, licensing, compute, and uncertainty; do not treat a single paper as settled truth. |

## Frontier and experimental methods

The AI may propose a newer or unusual teaching technique when a verified current source and a clear
mechanism make it relevant. Label it `experimental for this learner`, state the expected benefit and
cost, offer a normal fallback, and evaluate it through observed performance or learner experience.
Do not market novelty as effectiveness, silently self-modify the Skill, or weaken evidence rules.

`Feynman technique` is used here only as the familiar product label **Feynman-style teach-back**.
Its defensible mechanisms overlap self-explanation, retrieval, generative processing, and learning
by teaching; the label itself is not a universal scientific prescription.
